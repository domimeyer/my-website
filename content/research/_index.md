My research focuses on the formation and subsequent evolution of massive stars throughout their
entire life cycle, from their earliest embedded phases (accretion discs, magnetospheric accretion
columns) to their mature stage (stellar wind bubbles and bow shocks, shells) to their final compact
remnants (supernova remnants, pulsars and pulsar wind nebulae).  

I develop and exploit high-resolution fluid-dynamics simulations, coupled with radiative-transfer
and non-thermal emission modelling, in order to connect fundamental physical processes with multi-
wavelength and multi-messenger observables. A significant fraction of my activity therefore relies on
high-performance computing (HPC), large-scale data analysis and machine learning methods.  

The central goal of my work is to establish a coherent physical framework linking the initial properties
of stellar environments to the evolutionary pathways of massive stars through to their final pulsar phase.
By interpreting present-day circumstellar structures, I identify the physical conditions and feedback
mechanisms that shaped their evolution and govern the future development of their surrounding
interstellar medium.
